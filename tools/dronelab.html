DroneLab

Version 1.2

DroneLab provides steady, precisely tunable bagpipe-style drones and classic waveforms for instrument tuning, practice chanters, intonation training, and musical experimentation.

Starting and Stopping

Tap the drone puck to start or stop all enabled drones.

Select a Drone Sound to change the sound of all drones without changing their pitches.

The six bagpipe-style sounds are synthesized with different harmonic characteristics, from bright and buzzy to soft and mellow, and are inspired by the Bagpipe Custom Instrument Factory.

While they are named after actual styles of bagpipes, they are modeled, not sampled so may not match exactly the sound of the named set of bagpipes.

Sine Wave provides a pure reference tone, while Triangle Wave, Square Wave, and Sawtooth Wave offer additional harmonic sounds for tuning and experimentation.

Setting the Base Frequency

Select a note and octave as a shortcut to its equal-tempered frequency (A4 = 440 Hz), from C1 through C6.

Use Reset to Note to return to the exact shortcut pitch after editing the frequency.

Enter a frequency in Hertz directly, using up to three decimal places; valid frequencies are applied and saved as you type.

Use the minus and plus buttons to adjust the frequency by 0.1 Hz at a time, or hold either button for repeated adjustment.

All additional drones follow the base frequency, even when the base drone itself is disabled.

Invalid frequency entries leave the last valid pitch playing until corrected; press Escape or use Reset to Note to discard an invalid entry.

Adding Ratio Drones

Enable any of the three additional drones and select a just intonation interval or enter a custom ratio using two positive whole numbers.

The first number is the numerator and the second number is the denominator.

For example, with a base frequency of 240 Hz, a ratio of 3/2 produces 360 Hz and a ratio of 1/2 produces 120 Hz.

Custom ratios are applied and saved as soon as both fields contain a valid ratio; incomplete or invalid entries do not change the sounding pitch.

All enabled drones must have frequencies between 16 and 5000 Hz.

A pending custom ratio is checked again when the base frequency changes, and is applied if it becomes valid.

Press Escape in a ratio field to restore its last valid value.

Adjust each drone's volume independently, and disable any drone you do not need.

All settings can be changed while the drones are playing.

Saving and Loading Settings

Use Save Settings to save your current drone sound, base frequency, note and octave, and all four drone settings in a text file.

Correct any invalid frequency or ratio entries before saving.

Edit the suggested file name if you wish, then tap OK to save or Cancel to return to the tool.

On iPhone and iPad, choose Save to Files from the share sheet when available.

Use Load Settings to open a previously saved DroneLab text file and restore the complete configuration, including any disabled drones.

Loading a valid settings file stops playback, and you can tap the puck to start the newly loaded drones.

Playback and Settings

The drones are synthesized continuously without sample loop boundaries, and the displayed frequencies are rounded only for display when calculated from ratios or note shortcuts.

The audio hardware's clock determines the final physical pitch accuracy.

Your settings are saved in this browser when available, and playback always starts in the stopped state.

Playback stops when you leave or hide the page, or when audio is interrupted; tap the puck to restart it.

On supported devices, the tool requests a screen wake lock during playback and allows audio in iOS Silent Mode.
